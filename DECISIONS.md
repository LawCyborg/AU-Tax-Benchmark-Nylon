# Design decision register

Every design decision in this benchmark, the reason for it, the cost it carries, and where the reason is recorded. Each folder has a short `DECISIONS.md` listing the decisions that apply to it; this file is the single source of truth.

Each justification is stated in terms of what a reviewing tax practitioner needs, without reference to any system's output. **Status** is *Documented* where the reason is recorded in this folder (source given).

## Rubric

| \# | Decision | Justification | Known cost | Source | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | Origin of the rubric | Developed from conversations with five or more Nylon customers about what they value in research advice, and written by an independent contractor. Customer identities are confidential and are not disclosed. v1.0 is the first version of the rubric. Nylon's output format predates the rubric; the rubric was created independently of it, and Nylon's answers are known for ending with follow-on steps. The authors' view of any overlap between Nylon's format and the rubric is that it is coincidence, not causation: Nylon's product and the rubric each identified the same practitioner needs from practitioner insight | The practitioners consulted were Nylon customers, and cannot be named or contacted to confirm their input. No dated Nylon answer from before the rubric is in this folder. | Authors' statement, October 2026 | Documented |
| R2 | Grade as a reviewing partner grades a draft advice | The benchmark's purpose: whether AI research saves a practitioner time depends on whether its output can be checked and sent, not only whether it is right | Rewards answers written in a particular professional format | design-rationale §1, §1.4 | Documented |
| R3 | Verification primacy: a citation counts only if the retrieved text contains the supporting passage | Models cite real sections for propositions they do not contain, and pages they never opened; the only defensible test is whether the supporting words were in front of the writer | A system citing a page it saw only as a search snippet earns less, even when its claim is right | design-rationale §1.1 | Documented |
| R4 | Claim-level anchoring; set-level citation caps Citation grounding at 3 | A reviewer checks a proposition by going from the sentence to its authority; a judge that built the mapping would be grading its own research | Penalises bibliography-style citation regardless of source quality | design-rationale §1.2 | Documented |
| R5 | Primary-source primacy for statutory propositions | In Australian tax the Act is the law; ATO web guidance is not binding, not law, and often simplified or out of date | A correct rule stated from an ATO page is capped (Correctness level 2) | design-rationale §1.3 | Documented |
| R6 | Quote operative statutory words | A quotation is the check; a paraphrase must itself be checked against the section | Required for Correctness 5, Faithfulness ≥3 and Helpfulness ≥3, so it affects several dimensions at once. Nylon's lead in answers scored Correctness 5 (62% against 2–40%) reflects this requirement. | design-rationale §1.4, §2.3 | Documented |
| R7 | Workings table for computations | A reviewer checks a computation line by line; a figure without workings must be recomputed | — | design-rationale §1.4 | Documented |
| R8 | Engage and quote the leading case | Where a court has decided the point, the case is the authority | Required for Completeness ≥4 | design-rationale §1.4, §2.2 | Documented |
| R9 | Closing next step required for Helpfulness ≥3 | A draft advice is finished when it tells the reader what to do with it | Described by the authors as "the most contestable gate" | design-rationale §2.4 | Documented |
| R10 | Narration counts against Correctness and Completeness | Research narration is the writer's working diary, not advice; a reviewer would ask for it to be removed | Correctness and Completeness then reflect presentation as well as substance; the judge did not always separate narration from caveats | design-rationale §1.4 | Documented |
| R11 | Ladder rule: a level requires everything it names | Mirrors review: an excellent draft without the statutory text is not ready to send | Format traits gate substance scores | design-rationale §1.4 | Documented |
| R12 | "5 means nothing a reviewing partner would change" | Without the anchor, judges compress towards 4–5 | — | design-rationale §1.4 | Documented |
| R13 | Faithfulness levels as percentage bands | A count-like property; fixed bands make the judge count | — | design-rationale §2.3 | Documented |
| R14 | Weights 35 / 30 / 15 / 10 / 10 | An answer that cannot be verified cannot be relied on, so verification carries 65%; Correctness is partly enforced through Faithfulness and Citation grounding already | Correctness alone carries 15% | design-rationale §2.1, §2.6 | Documented; sensitivity in §5 |
| R15 | Rubric opening names NZ, AU and UK | Written for future use: NZ and UK runs are planned but have not been made, and the line tells the judge to limit itself to the jurisdictions the question set covers | No visible effect on any verdict | design-rationale §1.0; Authors' statement, October 2026 | Documented |

## Systems and runs

| \# | Decision | Justification | Known cost | Source | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S1 | Question text alone, no system prompt, for every system | No system was told what the rubric rewards | Comparison systems ran bare, while Nylon ran as a product that applies its own built-in prompting. | design-rationale §4.3 | Documented, except as S2 |
| S2 | Run Nylon as its standard service, but the comparison systems as bare models | Each system is tested as a user experiences it: the comparison systems as their providers' models with search, Nylon as subscribers use it. Output format is part of what is tested, not a side effect of the setup: each provider trains its model to a default answer format, and that default is what a practitioner receives unless they write their own instructions. Formatting is central to whether research is usable: a reviewing practitioner needs the operative statutory words, workings, the leading case and a next step (R2, R6–R9), and in the authors' view generic model output does not present research the way a practitioner needs. Producing that format is part of what a specialist product does, so giving the comparison systems instructions written to match Nylon's format would test a product the authors built, not the providers' models | Nylon's product applies its own built-in prompting; the comparison systems received no equivalent prompt | Authors' statement, October 2026 | Documented |
| S3 | One shared web-search and page-reading tool for the comparison systems | Differences then reflect the model, not the tools | Tool-call counts and token usage are not published, so S4 and T2 cannot be checked from this folder. Captures differ by system: of Gemini's 335 cited sources, 140 were neither read nor found in search and 68 were seen only as search snippets, which weighs on its Faithfulness; comparison page reads run to 1,000,057 characters, Nylon's own web reads to 40,001 | design-rationale §4.3; Authors' statement, October 2026 | Documented |
| S4 | Ceiling of 20 tool calls per question for the OpenAI systems only; Gemini 3.1 Pro, Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Opus 5.5 uncapped | 20 is the limit OpenAI's API sets on tool calls; no ceiling was set for the other comparison systems | Tool budgets differ by system: GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6 Sol could make at most 20 calls, while Claude Fable 5.1 and Opus 5.5 reached 30 and 29. GPT-6 Sol hit the ceiling on 52 of 91 questions and Astra on 7, so their scores may understate them. Nylon has no ceiling (max 28 calls). | README; design-rationale §4.3; per-question tool-call counts verified by the authors; Authors' statement, October 2026 | Documented |
| S5 | One answer per question | A practitioner asks once | Single-sample noise per system | design-rationale §4.3 | Documented |
| S6 | Systems run between 28 September and 1 October 2026, not together; Nylon completed last | Nylon's answers were delayed by problems with the Nylon API and with source capture. Re-running every system together was judged cost-prohibitive and unlikely to make a difference, because the questions fix the 2022–23 period | Systems did not face identical web content or provider conditions. Nylon's run ended up to three days after the comparison systems' (an ATO PDF in Nylon's q3.8 sources is stamped 1 October 2026, 09:04; every comparison system's stamp is 28 September; `scripts/checks.py` §4). Each Nylon answer was generated once, with no retries, and no one running Nylon could see the comparison systems' answers or verdicts before Nylon's answers were completed; this rests on the authors' statement | README; Authors' statement, October 2026 | Documented |
| S7 | Strip progress messages from Fable and Opus answers automatically; grade imperfect removals as they stand | Neither system should be penalised for text outside its answer; editing answers by hand would mean the authors changing them | About 12 answers kept narration or fragments and were marked down for it. In some, the removal also cut the opening of the answer (Fable q2.4, q2.9, q5.17 and Opus q1.3 begin with a stray "\*\*"; q5.17 begins inside a table, without its headline answer). The narration rule also caught statements of what was not checked (e.g. Opus q3.10 "I did not open those two sections"). Sensitivity (`scripts/checks.py`): excluding those 12 questions for every system, Nylon 4.657 and Fable 4.392 (published: 4.675 and 4.321); Nylon's lead over Fable narrows from 0.35 to 0.27. The authors regard the stripped answers as improved, not damaged, and the penalty as fair, because progress messages do not help a practitioner's review; they expect the raw answers would score worse. The raw answers are not published | design-rationale §4.5; authors' statement, October 2026 | Documented |
| S8 | Treat Nylon as a black box; use only its answer and cited sources | Nylon's harness design is confidential | Readers cannot inspect how Nylon retrieves, prompts or formats its answers | Authors' statement, October 2026 | Documented |
| S9 | Nylon's October Snapshot held fixed | Nothing in Nylon changed after the rubric was drafted, nor during the week of 28 September 2026. Nylon does not use Kimi K3 (G1) | Nylon's design, including its underlying models, is commercially sensitive and not disclosed, so this rests on the authors' statement | Authors' statement, October 2026 | Documented |
